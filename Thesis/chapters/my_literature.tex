\section{Related Work/Simulation Based approaches}
\label{sec:relatedwork}

The optimal camera placement problem is known as the camera network design problem (CND) in photogrammetry. CND in photogrammetry has been a problem that has been investigated for a couple of decades. \citet{grafarend1974optimization} in the field of geodesy sought to optimise geodetic networks by generalising the optimisation concepts of \cite{helmert1924ausgleichungsrechnung} and defined a classification scheme.  Whilst originally it pertained to the field of geodesy, photogrammetry professionals leveraged the classfication scheme and translated it to the interrelated problems of CND particularly highlighted in the work done by \citet{fraser1984network} This classified scheme for the the optimisation of geodetic networks was divided into four orders of design problems: zero order design (ZOD) problem, known as the datum problem is primarly to do with the choice of coordinate system for measurements. In CND optimisation this is the calibration step in which the world coordinate system is mapped out. The first order design (FOD) problem, the configuration problem, is the most complex in nature and persists to this day as finding the optimal configuration of the network. In CND has to do with the layout of the cameras in the network. The second order design (SOD) problem is the weighting problem or the densification problem. It asks, \textit{``What ought to be measured after designing an optimal configuration''}. It seeks to optimally distribute the observational work load. This translates to CND in how precise should the cameras be and what properties should they have such as: sensor resolution, the focal length and field of view to name a few. The third order design (TOD) problem also known as the bayesian approach or densification problem, asks \textit{what is the benefit of any additional measurement or observation in the network after reasoning about the quality of the present network?}. In CND, how does one improve the existing camera configuration by adding new cameras to the network. \citet{grafarend1974optimization} stated that it is impossible to optimise geodetic networks without reviewing optimisation algorithms and distinguishing between direct or simulation methods. This statement in part aligns with \citet{fraser1984network}'s statement that the precision of the network is a function of its geometry which is determined in the design stage. 
\cite{fraser1984network} mentioned that computer simulation has been implemented successfully in the optimisation of photogrammetric network design (PND) and stated that there are a number of ways that computer simulation can be used for PND. Utilising the classifcation scheme by \cite{grafarend1974optimization}, \cite{fraser1984network} put forth a general method for using computer simulation in the process of photogrammetric network design optimisation.

\cite{mason1995expert} implements or designs and expert system-based design the early terminology used for AI and mentions the observational redudancy, camera positioning and ray intersections for point triangulation. Expert System. 21 years on from the paper by \citet{grafarend1974optimization} the classification system is still being used to assist in PND however highlighting that the most complex of the design problems is the first order design, camera placement. The complexity of the problem is in the practically infinite number of camera poses even when working with a restricted environment.
