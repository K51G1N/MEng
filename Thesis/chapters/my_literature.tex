The optimal camera placement problem is known as the camera network design problem (CND) in photogrammetry. CND in photogrammetry has been a problem that has been investigated for a couple of decades. \citet{grafarend1974optimization} in the field of geodesy sought to optimise geodetic networks by generalising the optimisation concepts of \cite{helmert1924ausgleichungsrechnung} and defined a classification scheme. This classified the optimisation of networks into four orders of design problems: zero order design (ZOD) problem, which is primarly to do with choice of coordinate system for measurements, known as the datum problem. The first order design (FOD) problem is the one that persists to this day and it is the configuration problem. The second order design (SOD) problem is the weighting problem which asks the question \textit{what ought to be measured after designing an optimal configuration}. The third order design (TOD) problem also known as the bayesian approach or densification problem, which asks \textit{what is the benefit of any additional measurement or observation in the network after reasoning about the quality of the present network?}.
\citet{grafarend1974optimization} stated that it is impossible to optimise geodetic networks without reviewing optimisation algorithms and distinguishing between direct or simulation methods. 
The classification scheme by \cite{grafarend1974optimization} has been leveraged by professionals in the field of photogrammetry as the optimisation of a geodetic network leant itself to the optimisation of a camera network, known as multi-station photogrammetric network. 
\citet{fraser1984network} in particular utilised the classification scheme by \cite{grafarend1974optimization} in the optimisation of a photogrammetry multi-station network. He correctly identified that precision of the network is a function of its geometry which is determined in the design stage. He expanded upon the four orders of design problems and translated them into the context of photogrammetry (camera network design). The four orders of design problems are analgous a multi-camera configuration in the following ways.
The datum problem (ZOD) has to do with defining the world coordinate system and this is achieved by means of camera calibration. The configuration problem (FOD) has to do with the layout of the cameras in the network. The location (x, y, z) and rotation (pan, tilt and roll) of each camera. The weighting problem (SOD) has to do with the properties of the camera's such as the sensor resolution, the focal length and field of view to name a few properties. The densification problem (TOD) asks how does one improve the existing camera configuration by adding new cameras to the network.
\cite{fraser1984network} mentioned that computer simulation has been implemented successfully in optimisation of photogrammetric network design and stated that there are a number of ways that computer simulation can be used for photogrammetric network design. Utilising the classifcation scheme by \cite{grafarend1974optimization}, \cite{fraser1984network} put forth a general method for using computer simulation in the process of photogrammetric network design optimisation.
