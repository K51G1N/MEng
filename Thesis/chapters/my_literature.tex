The optimal camera placement problem known in photogrammetry as the photogrammetry network design problem. \citet{grafarend1974optimization} in the context of geodetic networks setup a general network design scheme. This classified the optimisation of networks into four orders of design problems: zero order design problem, which is primarly to do with choice of coordinate system for measurements, known as the datum problem. The first order design problem is the one that persists to this day as the one most focused on and it is the configuration problem. The second order design problem is the weighting problem which asks the question \textit{what ought to be measured once an optimal camera configuration has been found}. The fourth order design problem also known as the bayesian approach or densification problem which asks the question what is the benefit of any additional measurement or observation in the network given information about the quality of the present network.
\citet{grafarend1974optimization} states that it is impossible to optimise geodetic networks without reviewing optimisation algorithms and distinguishing between direct or simulation methods. 
The classification scheme by \cite{grafarend1974optimization} was leveraged by professionals in the field of photogrammetry as the optimisation of a network leant itself to the optimisation of a camera network, known as multi-station photogrammetric network. 
\citet{fraser1984network} in particular utilises the classification from \cite{grafarend1974optimization} in the optimisation of a photogrammetry multi-station network. He correctly identifies that precision is a function of the geometry of the network which is determined in the design stage of the network. He expands upon the four orders of design problems as mentioned by \cite{grafarend1974optimization} and translates them into the context of photogrammetry (camera network design). 
\cite{fraser1984network} mentions that computer simulation has been implemented succesffully in design optimistion for photogrammetric networks. Leveraging the classification scheme from \citet{grafarend1974optimization} and puts fourth a general practical simulation procedure to implement in computure simulation network optimisation.

One particular contribution of the paper by \cite{fraser1984network} is that they give a general flow of photogrammetric network design visualised in a flow chart based on their figure 3.
